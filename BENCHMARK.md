# Desktop meeting scorecard

Scored 16 Oct 2026 from the desktop UI in `run.html` (branch `cursor/qa-meet-desktop`), driven locally in Chromium, Firefox, and WebKit. Scores are 1–10. A lead means we are ahead of the best product that audience already uses, on that row, for the desktop call itself.

We lead the corporate average, **8.3** against Zoom and Meet at about 8. Polish is 8, so that lead is the average, not every row. We lead the church average, **8.1** against a church desk at about 8, on the average only: service ties a desk. We do not lead personal. A host can mute, hold, rename, and spotlight from the roster. Share screen is one dock button, and Alt+S does the same thing. Who, Invite, Run app, Vote, Password, Stage, Record, captions, Settings, Transcript, and Help each have a key, and the list names them. A shared slide fills the stage, with Stop sharing on the slide. A church broadcast has an order of service, and during Song the booth can read the current line and the next one, and the current line is on the picture the room sees. Settings, Who, Share, Vote, Password, and Invite follow German and Spanish. Coming back from another tab says you are still in the call. The Run app sheet, the file you download for the police, signing in as admin, Stop app, Turn chat off, and Save, Steal, Hide, and Talk on the app bar follow German and Spanish. Invite, Leading, and Full screen on that bar stay English.

## Who we are measured against

| Product | Where it still beats GifOS on desktop |
| --- | --- |
| Zoom | A gutter between gallery tiles, a waiting room that cuts every audio path, recording a company expects, and a few strings that stay English |
| Google Meet | Fewer controls, excellent captions, a composer that never fights the toolbar, companion / picture-in-picture that is obvious |
| Teams | Raise-hand and roster that stay readable in a large room, chat and files that feel like one place |
| FaceTime | Almost no chrome, names you already know, a call that feels like a room rather than a product |
| StreamYard / Restream | Scenes, lower thirds, branding, one “go live” a volunteer can teach in a minute |
| YouTube Live | Stream health, public chat at scale, a clear on-air state |
| Church streaming (ProPresenter, Resi, a typical “live service” desk) | Your own songs and slides, multi-camera, an archive of the sermon |

GifOS already beats them on a few things those products do not offer: no account, one link, peer-to-peer media, blur by default, and a quiet join. That is the trust row. It is not the whole call.
