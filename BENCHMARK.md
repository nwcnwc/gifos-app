# Meeting and broadcast mobile scorecard

Scored from the mobile pass in `QUIRKS.md`, not from a feature checklist. Scale is 1–5. A 5 means that audience would rather use this than the product that usually wins for them. We do not lead any audience yet.

Competitors this is held against: Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, FaceTime, StreamYard, YouTube Live, and church streaming desks (Resi, BoxCast, ProPresenter-style “go live” panels).

## Where they still beat us

- **Zoom / Meet / Teams.** The hang-up, mute, and camera controls stay inside the screen on a 320px phone. Chat is a sheet or a column that does not sit under the call dock.
- **FaceTime.** One primary action, huge targets, the preview is the screen. On a tall phone the picture is that screen, and a tap on it is Go live (Q59). Watch and the host key open from More. FaceTime still wins the call: one control, and almost nothing else on the glass.
- **StreamYard / YouTube Live.** The state is a banner now (Q14). The picture leads a phone before Go live (Q44). A quiet viewer reads that the service or stream has not started, and an emptied room reads that it has ended (Q54). A recording on the phone plays back (Q60). A speaker fills the phone, and the frame stays whole (Q61). A viewer who watched can watch it again (Q62). Someone who arrives after it ended still cannot.
- **Church desks.** A viewer’s join says they are seen only if they raise a hand and are brought on stage (Q52). A quiet service reads OFF AIR. Sunday morning sits on the picture, and a tap on the picture is Go live (Q59, Q46).
