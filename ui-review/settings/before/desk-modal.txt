Third-party APIs

Beyond OpenAI-shaped models, wire up any keyed API — Deepgram, a trading API, whatever. Give it a short name, its base URL, how it authenticates, and your key, then hit Test & save. GifOS tries the call directly first. Deepgram needs no proxy at all — GifOS speaks its WebSocket protocol natively, so your key goes straight to Deepgram. For other sites that block browser calls (most brokerages), it retries through a stateless CORS proxy (https://cors-proxy.gifos.app) and remembers that. Honesty note: requests routed that way pass through that proxy in flight — it stores and logs nothing, but if you’d rather nothing of yours transit GifOS infrastructure, put your own proxy URL under Advanced (the one-file Worker is in the repo, cors-proxy/). It only saves if a test succeeds. Your key travels only toward that API’s own host (directly or via the proxy you chose), not to the app, and stays out of a shared backup.

Payments

Everything any app has charged on this computer, and what each one is allowed to ask for. Every payment here was approved by you on a GifOS payment screen naming the app’s verified author — apps never see your card, wallet, or balance, and what you bought is recorded here (not inside the app), so sharing an app’s GIF never shares a purchase. Payments are final; “forgetting” a purchase only clears it from this computer. The ceiling is the most one charge from that app may ask for.

Meetings that scale: nothing a phone pays grows with the room
NEW
Big meetings: your phone now hears only its own section of up to 25 people. What the whole room needs — the count, raised hands, who is on Stage, votes, the shared app — arrives as a small summary folded up and down the tree. A room of a thousand costs each phone the same as a room of twenty.
